## Request budget and throughput — arithmetic, not permission

For the demonstrated static calendar path, the expected sequence is:

1. GET /CaseSearch.
2. GET required stylesheet.
3. GET required script.
4. POST /CaseSearch.
5. GET one exact result-linked report action.

Order of the two assets can differ. With two required asset transmissions and
no additional uncached dependencies, **five actual requests** consume 5/8 of
the permit, 5/30 hourly and 5/120 daily capacity; three unused permit slots do
not become permission for extra PDFs. General count: **2 + A + D**, where A
is every actually transmitted asset/dependent request and D is documents
attempted (D <= 1 for SINGLE). Failed preflight, no eligible row, explicit
empty results, budget/quota/latency/identity stops can reduce the count or
prevent the PDF. Changed uncached assets can increase it until the fixed
permit cap stops admission. Count the ledger, not this forecast. Cached
responses are not new transmissions.

For planning only, assume a fresh calendar per hypothetical five-document
job, all five result-linked documents exist, and four calendar/asset requests:

| Documents | Five-document jobs | Calendar/asset overhead | Document GETs | Total requests |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 1 | 4 | 5 | 9 |
| 50 | 10 | 40 | 50 | 90 |
| 200 | 40 | 160 | 200 | 360 |

Nine requests fit a hypothetical job with requestCap 9–12, not the eight-slot
SINGLE proposal. Under shared 30/hour and 120/day, at most three such full
jobs (15 documents, 27 requests) fit one hourly capacity window, and thirteen
(65 documents, 117 requests) fit a daily capacity window, with unused
capacity potentially usable only by separately approved smaller jobs.
Fifty documents need at least three hourly capacity windows (last window no
earlier than two hours after the first if started empty) and fit one daily
capacity window. Two hundred need at least twelve hourly capacity windows
and three daily capacity windows (last daily window no earlier than 48 hours
after the first), **plus request duration/pacing/queue time**. Rolling quotas
do not reset at a wall-clock hour or midnight. Other source traffic consumes
the same capacities; nine-request and 360-request forecasts are not measured
live throughput or upper bounds on calendar overhead.

If only separately admitted SINGLE jobs were used, each would require a fresh
calendar: 5, 50, 200 documents would instead cost **25, 250, 1000** requests
at the same four-request overhead. That difference must not be hidden.
The existing CLI SMALL stage is three documents; MODEST is ten and exceeds
the unchanged default five-document policy, though the pre-existing 200-record
override would technically admit it. That is not owner authorization. A five-document batch is not an
automatically authorized next stage. No limits or environment values changed.

## Every pilot proof predicate, including timing

`pilot.ts:328–335,399–402` requires all of:

1. Not bounded, not inspection, not calendar-only.
2. Persisted terminal job status COMPLETED.
3. `calendarComplete` true.
4. `validDocuments === recordCap`; validDocuments counts EXTRACTED or
   MISSING_BAIL_SECTION items, not HTTP-200 responses or matching low-bail rows.
5. Zero non-OK request outcomes.
6. Nonempty accounted request list.
7. Ordered requests satisfy timingValid: every request after the first starts
   no earlier than previous completion plus **discoveryPolicy.spacingMs**;
   previous completion must exist.
8. Every request, not just the PDF, has non-null `durationMs < 20_000`.
9. No active source request.
10. No source latencyWarning.

**A document request slower than 20 seconds fails pilot verification by
design, even if a valid PDF was downloaded. Exactly 20 seconds also fails:
the comparison is strictly `< 20_000`.** The HTTP timeout remains 45 seconds;
that is a transport bound, not the pilot's success threshold. Calendar/assets
can fail this predicate too.

An additional timing mismatch is present and was not changed: the verifier
uses the single discoveryPolicy.spacingMs (default navigation 1000 ms), but
the scheduler uses operation-specific asset spacing (default 250 ms)
(`lib/research-pacing.ts:13–24`, `discovery-scheduler.ts:138–141`).
A scheduler-lawful fast asset gap can therefore fail timingValid. Retained
backoff can increase gaps, but must not be assumed. Report that specific
failed predicate rather than claiming a successful PDF proves the pilot.
Do not change pacing or the verifier to force this proposal through.

Capture-to-GET elapsed seconds are separate from durationMs. A fresh run
records the monotonic instant when validated actions are captured and
measures after the document grant, just before fetch. This includes waiting
for grants/pacing/earlier documents, not the calendar POST start. Resumed
actions without a genuine capture instant report null, not an invented age.
There is no established link lifetime or acceptable elapsed-seconds limit.

On any failed predicate report it explicitly, retain ACCESS_DENIED and all
request history, and stop. A one-PDF success is not pilot proof, acceptance
A1–A7, approval for collection, or permission to publish.
